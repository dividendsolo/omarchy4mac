---
title: "Hyprland on a Mac: what runs, and what cannot"
description: "Hyprland is Linux and Wayland only, so it does not run on macOS. AeroSpace, SketchyBar and Hammerspoon give a Mac the same behaviour and keys."
---

Hyprland does not run on a Mac. It is a Wayland compositor, Wayland is Linux's
display protocol, and macOS is not Linux. There is no build of it for macOS and
there is not going to be one, so "Hyprland on macOS" means one of two things:
run Linux on the Mac, or reproduce the behaviour with tools macOS does have.

omarchy4mac does the second one, and it is the reason the repo exists. It takes
Omarchy, DHH's opinionated Arch and Hyprland setup, and rebuilds the parts that
matter with native macOS tools:

| Omarchy piece | What omarchy4mac runs | Config |
|---|---|---|
| Hyprland | AeroSpace | `aerospace/aerospace.toml` |
| Waybar | SketchyBar | `sketchybar/` |
| Window borders | JankyBorders | `borders/` |
| Keybindings and menus | Hammerspoon | `hammerspoon/` |
| Omarchy themes | the `theme` script, synced from Omarchy | `bin/theme` |

```sh
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dividendsolo/omarchy4mac/main/install.sh | bash
```

## What you keep

The keybinding vocabulary is Hyprland's. Five workspaces, ⌥1 to ⌥5, windows
carried between workspaces and monitors with ⌘⇧ and the arrow keys, ⌥F to fill
the screen, ⌥A for the accordion layout, ⌥S to float a window, and ⌘⇧; for the
service mode that joins and flattens windows. The repo's own
[AeroSpace config](/docs/aerospace-config) is the file, and
[Keybindings](/docs/keybindings) is the table.

The bar is Omarchy's Waybar layout: workspaces and the logo on the left, the
clock and the weather in the middle, the tray and the system items on the
right. [SketchyBar config](/docs/sketchybar-config) walks through it.

The themes are Omarchy's own. `theme --sync` pulls the theme definitions from
the Omarchy repository at the commit pinned in `OMARCHY_COMMIT` and renders
Ghostty, Neovim and btop from Omarchy's templates, so every theme Omarchy ships
is a theme here. [Themes](/docs/themes) has the commands.

## What you lose

Hyprland features that AeroSpace has no equivalent for: groups, the scratchpad,
pinning a window, per-window opacity and keybindings, and toggling the gaps at
runtime. Annotation and the `hyprctl` scripting surface have no port either.

Two more things are different by nature. Hyprland is a compositor, so
animations, blur and rounded corners belong to it; on a Mac the windows are
drawn by macOS itself, so the rounding is the system's and the transparency
reaches the terminal, the bar and the borders only. And Hyprland follows no
system appearance, so light and dark is a macOS setting that omarchy4mac
watches instead.

[How close is this to real Omarchy?](/docs/parity) scores the port item by
item, at 75 per cent of Omarchy v4.5.0 today, and the
[parity checklist](/docs/parity-checklist) is the full list, ticked or not.

## The other route

If you want the real thing, the Mac can run Linux: Asahi Linux on Apple
silicon, or a virtual machine. Both give you Hyprland itself. Neither keeps
your macOS apps, the Mac's own keyboard handling, or the Terminal, and that
trade is the whole reason a port is interesting. omarchy4mac installs nothing
of Arch and nothing of Linux, and leaves the Mac running macOS.

[Native macOS tiling](/docs/tiling-window-manager-mac) is the same choice, seen
from the window manager's side.
